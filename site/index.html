Yulong Liu

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Earth Science at Cornell University, advised by Chloé Arson. I am in the Department of Earth and Atmospheric Sciences, with minors in Computer Science and Scientific Computing.

Research My research connects computational geomechanics and scientific machine learning. I combine coupled multiphysics simulation with physics-informed neural networks and operator learning to study subsurface and reservoir systems.

In rock mechanics, I develop physics-informed neural networks for pressurized cavities with complex geometry and heterogeneous material properties. In geothermal systems, I study operator learning for fractured injection-production reservoirs, using high-fidelity simulation ensembles to build fast surrogate models.

I also work on PDE-grounded intent verification for LLM-generated multiphysics simulation code: checking whether a simulation solves the intended physical problem, beyond whether the code runs.

Research interests
Computational geomechanics and poromechanics; physics-informed learning and neural operators; geothermal reservoirs and porous media; reliable AI for scientific simulation.

I received my B.S. in Mining Engineering from Northeastern University in 2024. My undergraduate thesis studied deep learning for microseismic signal classification and arrival-time picking.
