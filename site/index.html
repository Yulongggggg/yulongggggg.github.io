Scientific AI · Multiphysics Geomechanics

Yulong Liu

I study differentiable programming, hybrid neural PDE solvers, and uncertainty-aware scientific AI for multiphysics geomechanical systems.

My work brings learning into numerical simulation to understand how fluid flow, heat transport, and rock deformation interact. I am interested in models that are efficient to evaluate, can be calibrated from observations, and make uncertainty part of their predictions.

At Cornell, I work with Chloé Arson as a Ph.D. candidate in Earth Science, with minors in Computer Science and Scientific Computing. My projects span neural operators for geothermal reservoirs, physics-informed modeling of heterogeneous rock, and verification of AI-generated simulation code.

Before Cornell, I earned my B.S. in Mining Engineering at Northeastern University in 2024, where I worked on deep learning for microseismic signal analysis.

Research directions

01

Differentiable programming

Connecting physical simulation with learning and inverse problems through differentiable models.

02

Hybrid neural PDE solvers

Combining numerical structure with neural representations to model coupled processes in rock and reservoirs.

03

Uncertainty-aware scientific AI

Studying how uncertainty propagates through multiphysics predictions and how to assess their reliability.
